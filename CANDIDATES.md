# Local judging — candidate words for "The Word You'd Delete From the Dictionary"

Judged against the judge's brief, criteria in order:
1. A real word a reader recognises.
2. Funny at first sight — the joke lands the moment the word is gone, no caption needed.
3. The humour comes from the word itself (sound, double meanings, the gap it leaves), not shock.
4. Fresh — not the field's first thought, still instantly understood.
5. Exact: one word, spelled correctly.

Field check (25 Nov 2026 ~14:15 WIB, 353 submissions): titles naming a word
were treated as taken. Taken by title: irregardless, guesstimate, moist,
queue, colonel, tomorrow, skibidi, whatchamacallit, thingamajig, doohickey,
sesquipedalian, defenestrate/defenestration, gobbledygook,
floccinaucinihilipilification, kerfuffle, shenanigans, onomatopoeia,
supercalifragilisticexpialidocious, dictionary, erase, delete, discombobulate,
pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis, phlegm, actually, allegedly,
wrong, pants, fine, whom, literally, lisp, procrastinate, monday, deadline,
unsubscribe, irony, awkward, indelible, inflammable, delve, no.
Blind rounds hide entry texts, so a generic-titled entry could still hold any
word; the title scan is the best available proxy.

## Candidates judged

### 1. mnemonic — SUBMITTED (winner of the local judging)
- (1) Universally recognised: every English speaker has met it in school.
- (2) Lands in one beat: the word for remembering is the word nobody can
  spell — it opens with a silent m. Delete it and the language loses its
  memory-trick word while nobody can miss spelling it.
- (3) The humour is the word itself: a memory aid you cannot remember, a
  spelling trap about spelling. Self-referential irony, no shock.
- (4) Fresh: not among the field's first picks (moist / irregardless /
  queue / colonel / supercalifragilistic all taken); a spelling-trap pick
  nobody titled.
- (5) Exact: 8 chars, one word, correctly spelled.

### 2. bumfuzzle (runner-up)
- Funny sound, means "to confuse"; deleting it leaves everyone bumfuzzled
  with no word for it — the same self-irony as mnemonic. Loses on (1):
  far fewer readers recognise it, so the joke needs the meaning first.
  Mnemonic's irony needs no dictionary lookup.

### 3. flummox
- Same confuse-family irony, more recognised than bumfuzzle, fun sound.
  Loses to mnemonic on (2)/(3): the deletion joke is generic ("can't say
  I'm flummoxed"), while mnemonic's joke is specific to the word's own
  spelling trap.

### 4. snark
- Recognised, punchy; erase it and the internet loses its snark. The gap
  joke is real but thin: the humour is about the concept (snarky comments),
  not the word itself. Loses on (3).

### 5. ultracrepidarian
- Best gap joke in the field: delete it and confidently-wrong people lose
  their label. Loses on (1): most readers have never seen the word, so the
  joke never lands at first sight.

### 6. flibbertigibbet
- Delightful sound, recognised via musicals; but the deletion has no joke —
  losing a word for a flighty person is not funny. Loses on (2)/(3).

### 7. rizz
- One-beat gap joke (nobody has rizz anymore), instantly understood by the
  young, in the dictionary since 2023. Loses on (3)/(4): the joke is the
  concept, not the word, and slang this trendy reads stale by late 2026.

### 8. hornswoggle / lollygag / nincompoop / poppycock / malarkey / bamboozle
- All recognised, all funny-sounding. All lose on (2): there is no joke in
  their deletion, only a funny sound. A funny word is not a funny deletion.

### 9. tittle / aglet / widdershins / borborygmus / snollygoster
- Cute gap jokes (the dot over the i loses its name; the shoelace tip goes
  nameless), but all lose on (1): the reader has to know the meaning first.

## Rejected outright
- moist, irregardless, queue, colonel, whatchamacallit, defenestrate,
  sesquipedalian, gobbledygook, dictionary, erase, delete — already in the
  field by title; a byte-identical entry shares the rate and the earlier
  receipt places above it.
- supercalifragilisticexpialidocious, antidisestablishmentarianism-family,
  floccinaucinihilipilification — the cliché long-word picks; (4) fails.
- yeet — dated slang, thin deletion joke.
- petrichor — beautiful, not funny.

## Verdict
**mnemonic**: the only candidate that is universally recognised, lands its
joke in one beat with no explanation, draws the humour from the word's own
spelling trap, and is fresh in this field.
